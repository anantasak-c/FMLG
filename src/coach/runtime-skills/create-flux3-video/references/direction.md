# FLUX.3 direction notes

- Write one coherent shot or deliberately timed sequence that fits the requested duration.
- Prefer observable nouns and verbs over abstract mood adjectives.
- State the subject, immediate physical action, environment response, camera motivation, light, sound source, and final frame.
- Lock count, wardrobe, props, screen direction, scale, and supplied-image identity when continuity matters.
- Keep Thai dialogue short enough to speak naturally in its beat; preserve it exactly inside quotation marks.
- Put typography and precise graphics in post-production rather than asking the video model to render them.
- Audit conflicting camera directions, impossible motion, overloaded timing, discontinuous identity, unexplained audio, and generic negative prompts.

This directing framework adapts ideas from Serge Shima's Visual Skills under CC BY 4.0.
